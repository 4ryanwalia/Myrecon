How much do OSINT tools cost? Prices checked 15 November 2026

Short answer

OSINT tools cost anything from nothing to about US$70,000 a year for the largest published API plan. Open-source scanners such as Sherlock, Maigret and SpiderFoot are free. Breach and lookup services start at about US$3 to US$10 (LeakCheck: US$2.99 for 24 hours or US$9.99 a month). Investigation platforms such as Maltego and Intelligence X start at EUR 3,000 a year, and enterprise suites such as ShadowDragon are quote only.

Method: every price on this page was read from the vendor's own pricing page or repository on 15 November 2026. Nothing was taken from review sites or AI summaries. MyRecon publishes this page and sells one of the tools listed.

Search for "how much do OSINT tools cost" and most results are top-ten lists with no prices in them. This page does the opposite: a dated price table, the cost of a single search where a vendor publishes enough to work it out, and the costs that never appear on a pricing page. If a price below is out of date, the source link in each row goes to the page we read.

Cheapest and most expensive OSINT tools

Cheapest overall: the open-source tools. Sherlock, Maigret and SpiderFoot are MIT-licensed and cost nothing to run on your own computer. The price you pay is setup time, Python dependencies and keeping them updated. The WhatsMyName dataset is also free, but its project was archived on 9 November 2026, so its site checks are no longer maintained (what that means for users).

Cheapest paid option: LeakCheck Basic at US$2.99 for 24 hours of breach lookups, with no renewal. For a monthly plan, MyRecon Pro was listed at US$3.99 a month on 15 November 2026, and Have I Been Pwned Core 1 works out to US$4.39 a month on annual billing.

Most expensive published prices: Have I Been Pwned's largest High RPM plan is billed at US$69,996 a year, for an API allowance of 24,000 email searches a minute. Intelligence X Enterprise is EUR 20,000 a year, and Shodan Corporate is US$1,099 a month (US$13,188 a year). Enterprise investigation suites such as Social Links, Skopenow and ShadowDragon publish no prices at all, so the only way to compare them is a written quote.

What one search actually costs

Plan prices hide the number that matters: what you pay for each lookup. Where the vendor publishes both a price and an allowance, the cost per unit is below. Treat these as ceilings on value, not promises. A "credit" on one service can cover a single API call, while on another it covers a whole multi-source search.

The pattern is consistent across the market. Short passes and small plans cost the most per search. Big allowances look cheap per unit, but only if you actually use them. An investigator who runs ten searches a month pays more per search on a large plan than on a pass.

Costs that are not on the pricing page

  • Seats. Maltego bills its Professional tier per seat, for up to five users.
  • Annual billing. Maltego and Intelligence X only show yearly prices. Hunter.io's yearly price is 30% lower than monthly, but you pay twelve months up front.
  • Credits that run out. Credit plans stop working when the allowance is spent. Check whether unused credits roll over: OSINT Industries says extra credits you buy do not expire monthly, and Hunter.io says its bulk credits last 12 months.
  • Your time. Free command-line tools need installation, updates and someone who can read the output. Count staff time as a cost: for free tools it is often the largest one.
  • Verification. No tool on this list proves who owns an account. Every result still needs a person to open the source and check it. Our verification guide explains how.

Prices we could not verify

We left out prices we could not read on the vendor's own site on 15 November 2026. People-search subscriptions such as Spokeo, BeenVerified and Whitepages refused automated page reads and show their prices at checkout. DeHashed's pricing address returned a "page not found" error. Snusbase states no price on its home page. Censys, Social Links, Skopenow and ShadowDragon sell by quote. If you need one of these, ask the vendor for a written quote that states the seat count, the credit allowance and the renewal terms.

Frequently asked questions

Are there free OSINT tools?

Yes. Sherlock, Maigret and SpiderFoot are free and open source under the MIT licence. Have I Been Pwned offers free browser breach searches, Maltego has a free Basic plan, and MyRecon gives signed-in users five free Standard username scans a day.

What is the cheapest paid OSINT tool?

Of the prices published on 15 November 2026, LeakCheck Basic is the cheapest at US$2.99 for 24 hours. The cheapest monthly plans listed that day were MyRecon Pro at US$3.99 and Have I Been Pwned Core 1 at US$4.39 a month on annual billing.

What is the most expensive OSINT tool?

The highest published price we found was Have I Been Pwned's High RPM 24000 plan at US$69,996 a year. Intelligence X Enterprise is EUR 20,000 a year. Enterprise suites such as Social Links, Skopenow and ShadowDragon do not publish prices.

Why do OSINT tools charge in credits?

Each lookup costs the vendor money in data licences, proxies and compute, so credits tie the price to usage. Compare tools by cost per credit and by what one credit buys, because a credit means different things on different services.

How current are these prices?

Every price was read from the vendor's own page on 15 November 2026. Vendors change prices without notice, so follow the source link in each row before you buy.
