Who this is for, and who it is not for

There are good reasons to look someone up. You are about to rent from a private landlord, buy an expensive item from a stranger, meet someone from a dating app, hire a contractor, or reply to an investor who emailed out of nowhere. Checking that the person is who they say they are is sensible, and many safety organisations recommend exactly that before a first date.

The other good reason is yourself. The most useful deep search you will ever run is on your own name, because it shows you what a scammer, an employer or a harasser would see.

This list is not for keeping tabs on an ex, monitoring a partner, or finding someone who has cut contact with you. That is stalking, it is a crime in most countries, and none of these tools reveal private accounts or locations anyway. If you are the one being watched, skip to our doxxing prevention guide.

How to verify a person in 15 minutes

  1. Start with what they told you. Name, city, workplace, handle, photos. Write it down.
  2. Run a deep search on the name plus city, and separately on the handle.
  3. Reverse search two photos. One profile photo, one casual photo.
  4. Check consistency. Does the LinkedIn job match what they said? Is the business site older than last month on the Wayback Machine?
  5. Stop when you have your answer. Verification needs a yes or no on identity, not a dossier.

Same-name confusion is the most common mistake. Two signals that match (the same photo and the same employer, say) are worth more than ten pages with the right name. Our guide to finding public social accounts ethically goes into how to tell them apart.

Frequently asked questions

What is a deep search tool?

A deep search tool runs many targeted queries at once, such as site restrictions, exact phrases and file types, and keeps only the results that match. It finds public pages a single ordinary search misses. It does not reach private accounts or hidden databases.

Can I find out everything about a person online for free?

No, and you should be wary of any site that promises it. Free tools find what is public: profiles, mentions, archived pages and images. Paid people-search sites mostly resell records that may be outdated or about someone else with the same name.

Is it legal to search for information about someone?

Searching public information is legal in most places. Using it to harass, stalk, threaten or discriminate against someone is not, and many countries have specific stalking laws that cover online monitoring. Keep searches proportionate to a real safety or verification need.

How do I see what others can find about me?

Run the same searches on yourself: your full name in quotes, your usernames, your email and your photos. Then remove or lock down what you do not want public, starting with old accounts and data broker listings.
