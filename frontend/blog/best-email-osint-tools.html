The 10 Best Email OSINT Tools in 2026, Ranked

Updated 15 November 2026 · MyRecon editorial

Quick answer: for one email address, start with MyRecon (breach history plus public profiles tied to the address, free, with sources) and confirm breaches on Have I Been Pwned. For Google accounts, Epieos and GHunt go deepest. For a whole company domain, use Hunter or theHarvester.

Publisher disclosure: MyRecon publishes this list and ranks itself first. Our criterion is the person investigating a single address who wants a free answer with sources, on a phone or in a browser, without sending sign-up probes to dozens of sites. Have I Been Pwned holds the most complete breach catalogue and we link to it from our own results. No tool paid to appear and there are no affiliate links.

The three questions email OSINT answers

Email OSINT sounds like one task but is really three, and tools specialise:

  • Has this address leaked? Breach search engines match the address against known data breaches and tell you which services lost it and what else leaked with it.
  • What public accounts are tied to it? Some services display the address or a hash of it publicly: Gravatar profiles, public Git commits, verified PGP keys. Others reveal whether an address is registered through their sign-up or recovery flow.
  • Who is behind the domain? For business addresses, the domain's DNS records, mail setup and staff email pattern tell you whether the address is real and how the organisation is set up.

We ranked tools on how well they answer those questions with evidence you can open, whether they avoid touching the target's accounts, and how much setup they need.

The 10 best email OSINT tools

#1 email OSINT tool

1. MyRecon: breaches and linked public profiles in one check

Enter an address on the web and MyRecon pulls breach history from two independent sources (LeakCheck and XposedOrNot), de-duplicates the breach names, and adds a timeline and exposure score. It then lists services linked to the address from public evidence only: named breaches, Gravatar and the accounts it verifies, GitHub commit email and owner-confirmed PGP keys.

On Android, the app adds breach alerts that compare the public breach catalogue on your phone, so your address is not sent anywhere by default; the per-address check is a separate, clearly labelled switch. Either way, MyRecon deliberately does not fire sign-up or password reset requests at other sites, so nobody receives a "was this you?" email because you looked. A site missing from the list means "no evidence", not "no account", and the results say so.

  1. Open the email check at myrecon.xyz, or turn on breach alerts in the app.
  2. Enter the address and review breaches, dates and exposed data types.
  3. Open each linked profile, then check the domain's SPF and DMARC.

A sensible email investigation order

  1. Breaches first. MyRecon or HIBP. The breach list tells you which services the address used, often years back.
  2. Public profiles next. Gravatar, Git commits and PGP keys tie an address to names and photos without contacting anyone.
  3. Pivot to the username. The part before the @ is often a handle reused elsewhere. Run it through a username search app.
  4. Check the domain. A business address on a domain with no mail records or a week-old registration is a warning sign. Our SPF, DKIM and DMARC explainer covers this in plain English.
  5. Only then consider active checks, and only with permission.

If the address is yours, read how to check if your email was in a breach for the clean-up steps, and what a clean result really means.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best email OSINT tool?

For a quick, free answer with sources we rank MyRecon first, and we publish it: it combines breach history from two sources with public profiles tied to the address. Have I Been Pwned is the reference for breach data, and Epieos and GHunt go deepest on Google accounts.

Can you find every account linked to an email address?

No tool can. Sites do not publish their user lists. Tools infer links from breach records, public profiles that display the address, and in some cases sign-up or password reset responses, each of which misses accounts.

Is it safe to use tools that test sign-up or password reset forms?

Tools such as Holehe query registration or recovery pages. That can break a site's terms and some sites email the address owner. Use them only on your own addresses or with permission, and prefer evidence-only sources first.

Does a breach result mean my account was hacked?

No. It means the address appeared in a leaked dataset. Change the password used on that site, stop reusing it anywhere else, and turn on two-factor authentication.
