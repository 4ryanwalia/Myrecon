Best Data Breach Checkers for Email and Password Alerts

Updated 28 November 2026 · MyRecon editorial · How we check facts

Use Have I Been Pwned or Mozilla Monitor for breach alerts tied to your email. For stolen-password warnings, start with the password manager you already use. To get new breach announcements on Android without providing an email address, use MyRecon's public catalogue alerts. Check a specific email separately to see whether it appears in breach records.

MyRecon publishes this guide and makes one of the products below. We compared the purpose of each check, the data it uses and the action each alert supports. Choose the alert you need

  • Email found in a breach. A provider found that address in an indexed breach. Open the affected service directly and check its notice for the exposure date and data types.
  • Saved password compromised. The password manager can point you to a saved login to update. Start there, then change the password on any other account where you reused it.
  • New breach announced. A company or product you recognise appeared in a public catalogue. Check your own address or wait for the provider's notice before treating your account as affected.
  • Unfamiliar login. Review sessions and security alerts inside the affected account. This is where you can check for possible account takeover and revoke access.

What triggers a breach alert on each service?

Email services alert when your address appears in a breach they newly add. Password managers alert when a saved password turns up in known leak data. Where do other breach checkers beat MyRecon?

For most people a free email alert from Have I Been Pwned, Mozilla Monitor or XposedOrNot is the better first step, because it works on any phone through the inbox and needs no app. MyRecon's alerts are Android only.

  • Password managers (Google, Apple, 1Password) check the passwords you actually saved. MyRecon cannot see your saved passwords.
  • Have I Been Pwned sells an API with published prices from US$4.39 a month (Core 1, read 28 November 2026). MyRecon publishes free breach metadata but no email lookup API.
  • XposedOrNot shows breach details and data types on its own site for free. MyRecon's web lookup checks one address at a time.

Which breach alerts work on a phone?

For iPhone, start with the Passwords app for warnings about saved logins and choose verified email alerts if you also want address monitoring. On Android, Google Password Manager checks your saved passwords. MyRecon adds new public breach announcements and a separate opt-in email monitoring feature. Email notifications from services such as Have I Been Pwned and Mozilla Monitor can reach either phone through your inbox.

What MyRecon sends for each check

  • Android catalogue alerts. The app downloads public breach information and checks for new entries on your device. The catalogue request needs no watched address. An alert here means there is a new announcement to review.
  • Android email monitoring or a one-off web email check. The selected email is sent to breach data providers to look for matching records. Android email monitoring is a separate choice from catalogue alerts.
  • Web password exposure check. The browser hashes the input locally and requests a padded response from Pwned Passwords using just the first five characters of the hash. The browser compares the rest of the hash locally. In our browser test, malformed or partial responses returned an unavailable result. MyRecon password privacy policy · Pwned Passwords documentation

What to do after a breach hit

  1. Open the service directly. Verify the breach name, date and data types against the provider's notice or account security page.
  2. Replace any exposed or reused password. Use a unique password or a passkey where you can. Review sessions, sign out unfamiliar devices and enable two-factor authentication.
  3. Check the date carefully if you changed the password recently. A provider may add an old breach to its catalogue long after it happened. Breach date vs. disclosure date

If one provider finds nothing, look at its coverage and whether the check completed. An error or rate limit leaves the question open. A completed search covers the records that provider indexed. How to read a no-match result · Step-by-step email breach check

Frequently asked questions

Which consumer apps monitor for stolen passwords?

Google Password Manager and Apple Passwords check saved passwords for known compromises. 1Password Watchtower checks items already in your vault. For a single password, use a trusted checker such as Have I Been Pwned's Pwned Passwords.

Which services send email alerts about new data breaches?

Have I Been Pwned, Mozilla Monitor and XposedOrNot offer free email breach alerts. Mozilla Monitor supports up to 20 addresses per account. MyRecon's Android app has separate catalogue alerts and opt-in monitoring for watched email addresses.

Can a breach alert tell me if a social account was taken over?

A breach alert reports known exposure. For signs of account takeover, open the social platform directly, review recent sessions and sign-in alerts, and use its recovery controls if activity looks unfamiliar.

Why do two breach checkers show different results?

Providers index different records, add them at different times and can restrict which results appear publicly. Treat an outage or rate limit as incomplete evidence. A completed no-match result covers only the sources that answered.
