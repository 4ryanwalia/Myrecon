How to Remove Yourself From the Internet: Complete Opt-Out & Deletion Guide

Updated 14 October 2026 · MyRecon editorial

If you search your own name online and find your phone number, home address, old forum comments, or forgotten photos staring back at you, you are not alone. Reclaiming your personal privacy is an achievable process. Here is the definitive, step-by-step guide to removing yourself from the internet in 2026.

On this page
  • Phase 1: Discover and Delete Inactive Accounts
  • Phase 2: Opt Out of People-Search Sites and Data Brokers
  • Phase 3: Remove Personal Information Directly from Google Search
  • Phase 4: Clean Up Public Social Media Profiles
  • Phase 5: Ongoing Privacy Hygiene

Phase 1: Discover and Delete Inactive Accounts

The average internet user creates dozens of accounts on websites they visit once and never return to. These dormant accounts are frequent targets for credential stuffing attacks and data leaks.

  1. Audit your usernames: Enter your childhood handles, email variations, and aliases into the MyRecon username search tool to scan 500+ platforms for forgotten accounts.
  2. Check password managers and email archives: Search your Gmail or Outlook inbox for terms like "welcome", "activate your account", "verify email", and "confirm registration".
  3. Delete, don't just deactivate: Deactivation preserves your data in the company’s database. Always navigate to Account Settings → Privacy/Security → Delete Account permanently. If a website lacks an automated deletion button, email their privacy officer invoking GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure) or CCPA deletion rights.

Read our in-depth comparison: Account Deletion vs Deactivation: What Actually Happens to Your Data.

Phase 2: Opt Out of People-Search Sites and Data Brokers

Commercial data brokers scrape public courthouse records, utility connections, and marketing surveys to sell detailed background reports containing your relatives, addresses, and phone numbers.

Under privacy laws, almost every major data broker provides a free manual opt-out process:

  • Spokeo Opt-Out: Visit Spokeo’s opt-out page, paste your listing URL, and verify the confirmation email.
  • BeenVerified Opt-Out: Search your name in BeenVerified’s opt-out portal and click the verification link.
  • Whitepages Opt-Out: Find your listing, submit the removal URL, and complete the automated phone verification call.
  • Radaris Opt-Out: Use their record removal control to delist public dossiers.

Pro Tip: Re-check these databases every 6 months, as data brokers routinely re-populate profiles when they purchase new marketing lists.

Phase 3: Remove Personal Information Directly from Google Search

Even if an original source is modified, Google’s search snippet may continue displaying cached personal information. You can request direct removals:

  1. Google's "Results About You" Dashboard: If you are signed into Google, access the Results About You tool to track search results showing your phone number, home address, or personal email.
  2. Google Outdated Content Removal Tool: If a webpage has already deleted your information but Google still shows it in search results, submit the URL to Google’s Remove Outdated Content tool to refresh the index immediately.
  3. Submit a PII Removal Request: For sensitive details like identification numbers, banking info, or doxxing threats, submit Google’s official Personally Identifiable Information removal request form.

Phase 4: Clean Up Public Social Media Profiles

For the accounts you choose to keep, minimize public exposure:

  • Switch accounts to private: Make your personal Instagram, X, and TikTok profiles private so only approved followers can view your content.
  • Audit privacy settings: Disallow search engines from indexing your Facebook or LinkedIn profiles directly in the settings menus.
  • Remove location tags: Delete geotags from past photos, check-ins, and bio descriptions.
  • Unlink third-party apps: Go to Google, Apple, and social login settings and revoke access to applications you no longer use.

Phase 5: Ongoing Privacy Hygiene

Privacy is not a one-time event; it is an ongoing habit:

  • Use email aliases: Use services like SimpleLogin, DuckDuckGo Email Protection, or Apple Hide My Email to generate disposable forwarding addresses for new websites.
  • Use a dedicated password manager: Never reuse passwords across services; generate random 20+ character passphrases for every single login.
  • Run periodic self-audits: Every six months, search your handles on MyRecon to verify that new public exposures haven't cropped up.

Use these checks for your own accounts or work you are authorised to do. MyRecon reports public-source results; a matching handle is not proof that two accounts belong to the same person. See our terms and privacy policy.
