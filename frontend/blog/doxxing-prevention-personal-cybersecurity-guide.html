Doxxing Prevention Guide: How to Protect Your Identity from Malicious Exposure

Updated 14 October 2026 · MyRecon editorial

Doxxing—the act of uncovering and publicly posting private details about an individual to encourage harassment, stalking, or real-world intimidation—is one of the most destructive threats on the modern web. Here is how attackers compile personal dossiers, and how you can systematically harden your digital footprint against them.

How Attackers Build a Doxxing Dossier (The OSINT Kill Chain)

Doxxers rarely possess advanced zero-day hacking tools. Instead, they exploit common privacy hygiene oversights across a predictable five-stage methodology:

  1. Handle Enumeration: The attacker takes your gaming or social handle and searches it across platforms using tools like MyRecon to find older, unmaintained profiles from years ago.
  2. Email & Breach Correlation: Once an associated email address is discovered, they query breach databases to see leaked plaintext passwords, security questions, or linked real names.
  3. Data Broker & Public Records Query: Armed with a city and real name, they query commercial aggregator directories (Spokeo, BeenVerified, Radaris) to find current home addresses, telephone numbers, and relatives.
  4. Metadata & Background Extraction: They inspect your photo uploads for EXIF GPS tags, unblurred street signs, school badges, or reflective surfaces that disclose physical location.
  5. Weaponization: The compiled dossier is posted to toxic forums or social media groups to direct harassment campaigns, swatting calls, or financial fraud.

Step 1: Eliminate Username Reuse Across Public Services

Username reuse is the single most common bridge connecting your anonymous online personas to your real-world identity:

  • Silo your identities: Never use the same username for professional networks (LinkedIn, GitHub) and casual or controversial platforms (Reddit, Discord, gaming servers).
  • Scan your exposure: Run a MyRecon username search on your current handles to see which services are publicly discoverable.
  • Delete legacy accounts: Close forgotten accounts from your childhood or teenage years using our account deletion guide.

Step 2: Scrub Your Home Address and Phone from Data Brokers

Anyone with internet access can purchase or browse your residential address unless you actively opt out:

  • Submit manual opt-out requests to primary people-search registries: Spokeo, BeenVerified, and Whitepages.
  • Request Google search delisting using Google’s Personally Identifiable Information Removal Tool for your home address and phone numbers.
  • Review property deed registries in your county if you own real estate; consider holding property in a privacy trust or LLC where permitted.

Step 3: Sanitize Image Metadata and Visual Backgrounds

Modern smartphone cameras embed rich metadata into every photograph unless stripped:

  • Disable camera location permissions: Go into your phone settings → Camera → Location → Never.
  • Strip EXIF data: Before uploading images to forums or cloud shares, scrub EXIF tags using metadata cleaners like ExifTool or mobile photo scrubbers.
  • Scrutinize visual backgrounds: Avoid posting photos showing exterior house numbers, distinctive window views, vehicle license plates, flight boarding passes, or package delivery labels.

Step 4: Protect Domain Names with WHOIS Privacy

If you own a personal website, blog, or portfolio domain, your registrar may publish your full legal name, home address, and telephone number in the global WHOIS database:

  • Check your domain using the MyRecon WHOIS & Domain tool to see what information is currently public.
  • Enable WHOIS Privacy Protection (offered for free by modern registrars like Cloudflare and Namecheap) to proxy your registration details behind anonymous forwarding handles.

Step 5: Lock Down Financial & Telecom Security (Anti-Swatting)

To defend against the most severe real-world harms:

  • SIM Lock / Carrier PIN: Contact your cellular carrier (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, Airtel, Jio) and set a strict verbal passcode or port freeze to prevent unauthorized SIM swaps.
  • Anti-Swatting Registry: If you are a high-profile streamer, journalist, or creator, many municipal police departments offer voluntary "anti-swatting" lists that require officers to verify threats before deploying SWAT teams to your address.

Comprehensive Defense Summary

Defending against doxxing is about reducing attack surfaces. When an attacker cannot connect your online handle to a real name, or your real name to a physical address, the chain breaks.
