No breach found: what the result does and does not mean

Updated 25 October 2026 · MyRecon editorial

“No breach found” means the completed check found no match in the sources it supports. It is not a certificate that your information has never been exposed. A timeout, unavailable provider or unsupported query is a different outcome and should remain unknown.

Read the result before taking action

OutcomeUseful interpretationNext step
Published breach matchA provider associates the queried identifier with a known recordRead the incident and exposed fields
Completed check, no matchNo match in the checked source at this timeRetain the date and source coverage
Unavailable or blockedThe check did not establish an answerRetry later or consult the provider
Company notification, no searchable matchThe notice and searchable catalogue cover different evidenceFollow the verified notice

Why a real notification may not appear in search

A catalogue can only expose records available to that service. Some incidents are not published there; some records use another address; some involve information that is not searchable by email. The Have I Been Pwned FAQ explains limitations of its service. Search coverage and notification coverage should not be treated as identical.

Use addresses you own and know you used. An account created with an alias can differ from the address you use today. Do not guess other people's addresses or upload private correspondence to make a check more complete.

Keep three questions separate

Was my identifier in a published breach? Does someone currently have access to my account? Is a public profile mine? These require different evidence. A breach match does not establish a current login. A username match does not establish account ownership. An unfamiliar session in your account settings is a reason to investigate even if your breach check is clean.

A repeatable review record

Keep a small private note with the source, date, outcome and next action. Avoid saving leaked data, screenshots of unrelated people or full notification letters in public issue trackers. A useful entry reads “completed source check, no match, checked 25 October; company notice reviewed separately.” It says what was checked without claiming complete safety.

Review unexpected security messages through the official service, update reused passwords through its own settings and inspect recovery options when appropriate. If a provider check failed, preserve that status until a later successful check resolves it. A green card should never hide a network failure.
