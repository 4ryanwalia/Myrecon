Reverse email lookup: find accounts and breaches linked to an email

Enter an email address to see the breaches that list it, the data each one exposed, and public profiles tied to it. Free, with no sign-in.

The lookup opens in the MyRecon tool and runs straight away. Nothing is saved to an account.

What the email lookup checks

Reported breaches
The address is checked against two free breach sources, LeakCheck and XposedOrNot. You see each named breach, its date where the source gives one, and the types of data it exposed, such as passwords, phone numbers or physical addresses. Large combined lists of old credentials are counted separately, so they do not inflate the number of distinct breaches.
Public profiles for the exact address
A public Gravatar profile and the accounts it lists, GitHub commits made with the address, and keys.openpgp.org, which only lists an address after its owner confirms it. These are things the owner of the address chose to publish.
Linked-account checks (optional)
Turn on "Also check linked accounts" and the lookup asks 100+ services, among them Spotify, Instagram, Pinterest, Amazon and Duolingo, whether the address is registered with them. This uses Holehe, an open-source tool, plus a few checks MyRecon maintains. It sends the address to every service it tests, so it is off unless you choose it.

Sensitive breaches, such as dating or adult sites that Have I Been Pwned marks as sensitive, are never listed against an address, and every result carries a note explaining that rule.

Holehe online, without installing Python

Holehe is an open-source command-line tool that checks whether an email address is registered on a service by reading how that service's sign-up or password-reset screen answers. Running it yourself means installing Python and the package, then reading raw output in a terminal.

The linked-account option in this lookup runs a pinned copy of Holehe on MyRecon's server, plus two checks MyRecon maintains for Hugging Face and HackerRank, and shows the answers as a list: registered, no signal, or could not check. Rate limits and blocks are counted as "could not check", never as "not registered". As with Holehe itself, each service it tests receives the address.

Find Google reviews by email

Some people leave public Google Maps reviews under a contributor profile tied to their email address. Deep Search takes an exact email, asks a public Google source for a contributor profile, and then collects the public Maps reviews for it: the place, rating, date, text and any owner reply that comes back.

Guests can preview one finding; an eligible paid account unlocks the full results. Coverage can be partial or capped, and no profile or reviews does not prove that the address has no Google account or activity.

How to run a reverse email lookup

  1. Enter the address. Use one you own or have permission to review. Paste it exactly; the lookup checks that address only, not variations of it.
  2. Decide on linked-account checks. Leave them off for a breach and public-profile check that contacts no one. Turn them on when you want registration signals and accept that each service sees the request.
  3. Read the result in three parts. Breaches tell you what was exposed and when. Public profiles tell you what the owner published. Registration signals tell you where the address appears to have an account.
  4. Act on it. Change any password that was reused on a breached service, turn on two-factor sign-in, and close accounts you no longer use. The email breach guide walks through each step.

How to read a registration signal

A registration check looks at how a service answers when asked about an address, usually on its sign-up or password-reset screen. If the answer differs for a registered address, that difference is the signal. It is good evidence that an account exists. It is not proof of who controls the account today.

Services change these screens without notice, and many rate-limit or block automated requests. MyRecon reports those as unresolved and counts them, so a partial run never reads as "no accounts". A service that is missing from the results means the lookup found no evidence there, not that no account exists.

What a breach result means for you

A breach entry means the address appeared in data that a service lost or that was later traded. The exposed data types matter more than the count. A breach that included passwords means any password you used on that service, and anywhere you reused it, should be treated as known. A breach that included phone numbers raises the risk of SIM-swap and text-message scams.

Breach dates are when the incident happened according to the source, which is often months before it was disclosed. For background on a specific incident, open the breach archive, which explains individual breaches from their published records.

Frequently asked questions

Is the reverse email lookup free?

Yes. Email lookups are free for every visitor and need no account. They do not use the username scan allowance, and MyRecon does not save the address to an account history.

What does a reverse email lookup show?

Reported breaches that list the address and the data types each one exposed, plus public profiles tied to it such as Gravatar and GitHub commits. With linked-account checks on, it also shows registration signals from 100+ services. Each result names its source.

Does the person who owns the email get notified?

Breach and public-profile checks do not contact the address. The optional linked-account checks send the address to each service they test, so a service could log the request. That is why the option is off by default.

Can it tell me who owns an email address?

Not by itself. A public profile, a breach record or a registration signal is a lead about the address, not proof of identity. Confirm anything important with an independent source before you rely on it.

Why does an address with no results still matter?

No result means the sources checked had nothing they could report. It does not prove the address was never breached or never used to sign up somewhere. Some services block automated checks, and those are shown as unresolved, not as clean.

Using email lookups responsibly

Look up your own addresses, or addresses you are authorized to review for security work. Do not use the results to harass, track or impersonate anyone. Read the terms of use and the privacy policy.
