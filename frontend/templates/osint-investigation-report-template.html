Free OSINT investigation report template

What goes in an OSINT report

An OSINT investigation report should contain eight parts: scope and authorisation, an executive summary with confidence for each answer, the starting selectors, the method and tools with dates, numbered findings that each point to an evidence item, analysis of alternative explanations, limitations, and conclusions. This template has all eight plus an evidence appendix.

Markdown opens in any text editor, pastes cleanly into Word, Google Docs, Notion or Obsidian, and keeps tables intact. Most report templates you find online are a PDF on a document-sharing site or a page that asks you to sign up. This one is a plain file you can keep, change and reuse, and it is free to adapt.

The eight sections and why each one is there

What each section of the template does.
SectionWhat to writeWhy it matters
1. Scope and authorisationWho asked, on what basis, which questions, and what is out of scopeShows the work was lawful and stops scope creep
2. Executive summaryA direct answer to each question with a confidence levelMost readers stop here, so it must stand alone
3. Starting selectorsEvery username, email, domain or name you began with, and its sourceLets a reviewer see whether a finding came from the brief or from your own pivot
4. Method and toolsTool names, versions and the UTC time each was runResults change as websites change; dates make them reproducible
5. FindingsNumbered findings, each tied to an evidence itemNo finding without evidence; no evidence without a finding
6. AnalysisHow findings connect and which other explanations you ruled outA matching username is not proof of identity
7. LimitationsUnavailable sources, unknown results, facts that could change the answerSeparates 'not found' from 'not checked'
8. ConclusionsPlain-language answers and next steps inside the authorisationTurns research into a decision

Use a confidence scale, and define it

Words like "likely" mean different things to different readers. The template ends with a four-level scale so every finding is graded the same way: Confirmed (verified at the source and independently supported), Probable (verified at the source, support not independent), Possible (a single unverified signal such as a matching username) and Unresolved. Put the scale in every report you send, even if the reader has seen it before.

The most common error in OSINT reports is grading a username match as Confirmed. In our own testing, six of 24 platforms returned identical responses for a real handle and an invented one, so a "found" result from any tool is a lead until you open the page. Our guide to verifying account matches covers the checks that move a finding up the scale.

A worked example

Say a small company asks whether a social account using its brand name belongs to a former employee. Section 1 records the request, the written approval and that contacting the account holder is out of scope. Section 3 lists the brand handle and the former employee's known work email. Section 4 logs a username scan, a breach check on the email and a manual review, each with a timestamp. Finding F1 might read: "The handle exists on three platforms (E1 to E3)", graded Probable. Finding F2: "The profile bio links to a personal site registered to a different person (E4)", which lowers the case for the original theory. Section 6 explains why the evidence points to an unrelated fan account, and Section 8 recommends a trademark report instead of an HR process.

Tips that make a report hold up

  • Capture evidence as you go, not at the end. Pages change and accounts disappear. The evidence log template has columns for the archive link and file hash.
  • Write times in UTC and say so. Mixed time zones are the easiest thing for someone to challenge.
  • Record negative results. "Searched 500 platforms, no account found on 15 November 2026" is a finding; leaving it out hides your coverage.
  • Keep personal data to what answers the question. A report is not a dossier.
  • Have someone else read the executive summary without the rest. If they cannot repeat the answer, rewrite it.

Frequently asked questions

What should an OSINT report include?

Scope and authorisation, an executive summary, the starting selectors, method and tools with dates, numbered findings tied to evidence, analysis of alternative explanations, limitations, and conclusions. A defined confidence scale and an evidence log should be attached.

How long should an OSINT report be?

As short as the questions allow. The executive summary should fit on one page; findings and evidence can run longer. Length is not quality: every finding should point to an evidence item.

Is this OSINT report template free?

Yes. Download the Markdown file, adapt it and use it for any lawful work. No sign-up is needed.

Can I open the template in Word or Google Docs?

Yes. Paste the Markdown into Google Docs or Word, or open the file in any editor that previews Markdown. Tables and headings carry over.
