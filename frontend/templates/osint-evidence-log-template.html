OSINT evidence log template (free CSV)

What an evidence log records

An OSINT evidence log records, for every item you capture, when it was captured in UTC, who captured it, the exact source URL, an archive link, the saved file name, a SHA-256 hash of that file, what it shows, and how confident you are. Those fields let someone else find, check and trust each piece of evidence later.

Download the evidence log (CSV, 1 KB)

The file opens in Excel, Google Sheets, Numbers and LibreOffice. It has fifteen columns and two example rows you can delete. It pairs with the investigation report template. The completed report example shows how a finding points to an evidence ID and retains any conflict between sources.

A worked example: checking an old handle

Suppose you are auditing where an old handle of yours still appears. A username scan reports a GitHub profile. Before logging anything, open the profile signed out and confirm it loads a real account page, not a login wall or an error page.

Take a full-page screenshot, save it as E1-github-handle.png, hash it, and submit the URL to an archive service. Row E1 records the capture time, the URL, the archive link, the hash and one sentence: "Public profile exists; display name and city shown."

Next you notice the profile links to a personal website. Open it, print the page to PDF and log it as E2, with E1 in its corroborated_by column and E2 in E1's. Neither row says "this is my old account". That conclusion goes in the report, where the confidence scale explains why two linked public pages make it Probable rather than Confirmed. If the website had been taken down, you would still add E2 and write "page removed, archive copy only" in the notes.

Ten minutes of logging like this means that months later you, or anyone you hand the case to, can see exactly what was visible, where and when, without trusting memory.

How to hash a capture

A SHA-256 hash is a fingerprint of a file. If anyone changes a single pixel, the hash changes. Record it right after you save the capture.

  • Windows (PowerShell): Get-FileHash .\E1-github-handle.png -Algorithm SHA256
  • macOS: shasum -a 256 E1-github-handle.png
  • Linux: sha256sum E1-github-handle.png

Store the original files somewhere read-only, and work from copies. The guide to protecting research notes covers storage and retention.

Rules that keep a log trustworthy

  1. Log at the moment of capture. Reconstructing a log afterwards is where wrong times and missing URLs creep in.
  2. Describe, do not conclude. "Profile shows the name Alex R. and the city Leeds" is evidence. "This is the subject's account" is a finding and belongs in the report.
  3. Archive public pages. Submit the URL to an archive service as well as saving your own copy. An independent archive is harder to dispute than your screenshot alone.
  4. Log what you could not capture. A login wall or a removed page is useful context. Write it in the notes column rather than skipping the row.
  5. Never log secrets. No passwords, session tokens or private messages, even your own. The log may be shared.

Where the evidence comes from

Most items in a public-source log start as a lead from a search tool: a username scan, a breach lookup or a search operator. A lead is not evidence until you open the original page and capture it. MyRecon results include the profile URL for each lead, and our evidence logging guide shows the full capture routine for a public profile.

Frequently asked questions

What is an OSINT evidence log?

A table that records every piece of evidence captured during an investigation: when, by whom, from which URL, the saved file, its hash and what it shows. Findings in the final report point back to its item IDs.

Why hash screenshots?

A SHA-256 hash proves a file has not changed since it was logged. If a capture is ever questioned, anyone can re-hash the file and compare.

Can I use this template in Google Sheets?

Yes. In Google Sheets choose File, Import, and upload the CSV. Excel, Numbers and LibreOffice open it directly.
