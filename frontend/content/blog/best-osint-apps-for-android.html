Quick answer: the best OSINT app for Android depends on the job. For finding where a username or email shows up, and whether it leaked in a breach, we rank MyRecon first. For networks use Fing or Network Analyzer, for images Google Lens, and for anything command-line, Termux.

Publisher disclosure: MyRecon publishes this list and ranks its own app first. The order is our editorial judgement for one kind of reader: someone on an Android phone who wants answers with source links, without installing a terminal or creating an account. If you are comfortable in a shell, Termux plus open-source tools will reach further than any single app, and we say so below. No app paid to be listed and there are no affiliate links.

How we picked and ordered these apps

Open-source intelligence on a phone is a different job from OSINT on a laptop. The screen is small, background work gets killed to save battery, and copying results into notes is clumsy. So the apps that earn a place here do one of two things well: they answer a common research question in a few taps, or they open the phone up to tools that would otherwise need a computer.

We judged each app on four things. First, what question it answers, stated plainly. Second, whether it shows its sources, because a result you cannot open and check is a rumour. Third, setup cost: an install-and-go app beats one that needs a Python environment, unless the extra power is the point. Fourth, how it handles uncertainty. A username checker that marks every site that answers "200 OK" as a found account looks impressive and is mostly wrong.

Everything here runs without root. Prices change, so we describe the cost model rather than quoting numbers that will go stale.

Termux

Termux is a full Linux terminal for Android. It is not an OSINT tool by itself; it is the reason a phone can run the OSINT tools that matter. Install Python and you can run Sherlock, Maigret, theHarvester or ExifTool exactly as on a laptop. Its maintainers recommend installing from F-Droid or GitHub rather than older store builds.

Which app should you start with?

  • Checking your own digital footprint: start with MyRecon. Search every username you have used, then run the breach check on your email. Our guide to finding your old accounts turns the results into a cleanup list.
  • Vetting a seller, landlord or online match: Google Lens on their photos first, then a username search on the handle they gave you. A reused stock photo is the most common red flag.
  • Suspicious link or QR code: MyRecon's QR checker, then Network Analyzer for the domain's age and host.
  • Learning OSINT properly: Termux, then work through Sherlock and ExifTool by hand. Understanding why a tool returns a false positive teaches more than any app.

How to read OSINT results without fooling yourself

Every tool on this list produces leads, not conclusions. A username that exists on twelve sites may belong to twelve different people. A breach result tells you an address appeared in a leaked dataset, not that the account was taken over. A photo match can be a reupload, a coincidence or a deliberate fake.

Three habits keep you honest. Open the original source for every result you plan to rely on. Look for two independent signals before linking accounts, such as the same profile photo and the same external website. And write down what you checked and when, because public pages change. Our explainer on username sweeps versus Deep Search goes deeper into why tools disagree.

Use these apps responsibly

These apps are built for checking yourself, protecting your family, verifying people you are about to trust, and authorized security work. Using them to monitor, track or harass someone is not research, and in most countries it is a crime. Frequently asked questions

What is the best OSINT app for Android?

For username search and breach checks without a terminal, we rank MyRecon first, and we publish it. For network work pick Fing or Network Analyzer, for images Google Lens, and for running command-line tools such as Sherlock use Termux.

Can I run Sherlock or Maigret on an Android phone?

Yes, inside Termux, which gives you a Linux shell with Python. Install Python, clone the project and run it as you would on a laptop. Expect slower runs and more blocked requests than a desktop, and keep the tool updated.

Do OSINT apps need root access?

None of the ten apps on this list needs root. PCAPdroid captures traffic through Android's VPN interface, and Termux runs in its own app sandbox.

Is it legal to use OSINT apps?

Looking at public information is legal in most places, but what you do with it matters. Harassment, stalking and unauthorized access are crimes regardless of the tool. Use these apps on yourself, your own assets or with permission.
