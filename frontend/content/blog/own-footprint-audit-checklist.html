A footprint audit works best as a short, repeatable checklist. It turns “I should clean up my online presence” into a set of actions you can complete and revisit. Use it for your own accounts or a footprint you are authorised to manage.

Make a private inventory

Write down the services and profiles you can confirm are yours. Check saved logins, old inboxes, password-manager entries and handles you previously used. If a public search surfaces a similar profile, mark it “unconfirmed” until you establish control through the provider's official sign-in or recovery flow.

A simple table is enough:

ServiceHandle or email hintActionFollow-up date
Example profilePrivate noteReview visibilityChoose a date
Old accountPrivate noteExport, then deleteAfter provider's waiting period
Example rows only. Keep the worksheet somewhere private and do not include credentials.

Prioritise the accounts with the most impact

  1. Secure your main email first. It can reset access to other services.
  2. Fix reused passwords. Give every active account a unique password and enable two-factor authentication.
  3. Reduce sensitive public details. Review phone numbers, precise locations, routines, workplace and family information.
  4. Close dormant accounts you control. Export data, cancel billing and move connected logins before requesting deletion.
  5. Handle copies separately. Ask the source site, data broker or search engine to remove material through its own process.

Track the result without creating a new risk

Record the action, request date and confirmation status. Do not put passwords, one-time codes, recovery answers, government identifiers or private messages in the worksheet. If you store notes online, protect that account with a unique password and multi-factor authentication.
