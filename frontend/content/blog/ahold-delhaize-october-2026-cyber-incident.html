Ahold Delhaize's October 2026 cybersecurity incident disrupted websites and mobile apps across several retail brands in Europe and the United States. An operational outage proves service disruption; it does not, on its own, prove that customer personal or payment data was exfiltrated.

What the company reported

The October 19 update from Ahold Delhaize (rechecked 28 November 2026) followed its October 12 disclosure. The company stated that malicious activity triggered precautionary shutdowns of selected systems across European and U.S. banners. Physical retail stores remained open while online ordering and loyalty apps were restored in phases.

Precautionary containment is standard incident-response practice: isolating internal networks stops lateral movement even when it temporarily takes e-commerce storefronts offline.

Was customer information stolen?

As of the October 19 update, Ahold Delhaize reported no evidence suggesting that sensitive customer, associate, or partner data had been taken, while noting that its investigation remained active. That is a time-bound statement rather than a permanent guarantee. Until forensic review closes or an individual breach notice is issued, accurate reporting classifies this event as an operational cyber incident rather than a confirmed customer-data breach.

How customers can check service updates safely

Major retail outages reliably attract opportunistic phishing and fake refund scams on social media, SMS, and email. Protect your account with four straightforward checks:

  • Go directly to official channels: Type the retailer's usual web address or open its official app instead of following links in social-media replies or unsolicited texts claiming to process outage refunds.
  • Watch for credential-harvesting lures: Legitimate service-status updates do not require re-entering payment-card details, bank logins, or one-time passcodes to restore a delivery or loyalty balance. Review CISA phishing guidance (checked 28 November 2026) for common impersonation indicators.
  • Keep receipts and monitor statements: Save order confirmations and review bank statements for duplicate or unauthorized charges during phased system recovery.
  • Use unique passwords on retail loyalty accounts: Retail accounts are frequent credential-stuffing targets. Check whether any password you reused on grocery or retail sites has appeared in unrelated older leaks using Pwned Passwords or MyRecon's local password exposure checker.
