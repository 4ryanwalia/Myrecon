A reverse phone lookup can match a displayed number to a public listing. Caller ID can be spoofed, so even a match to your bank leaves the caller’s identity unresolved. End a suspicious call and reach the organization using a number you already trust, such as the one on your card, statement or official app.

How to verify a suspicious call

  1. End the call. Keep account credentials and verification codes to yourself. If the caller claims to be helping with an account problem, ask only for the organization’s name and a general description of the issue.
  2. Find the organization’s contact information independently. Open an app you already use, a statement or the official site you know. If the caller claimed to be someone you know, contact them using a number already in your contacts.
  3. Ask the organization to confirm the claim. For a bank alert, ask the fraud team to check for the activity or case the caller mentioned. Keep the incoming caller ID, any callback number they gave you and the verified contact you used as separate details.

The FTC’s guidance on unexpected bank and government calls explains why a familiar number or an urgent request still warrants an independent callback.

Where to report an unwanted or fraudulent call

If you sent money or shared payment details, contact the payment provider or bank immediately through its official channel. Keep your receipt, the date and time, and any message or voicemail you received. Share evidence through the provider’s reporting channel.

  • United States. Use FTC ReportFraud for scams and the FCC’s unwanted calls and spoofing guidance for call complaints.
  • India. Use Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi to report suspected fraud calls and messages. If you already lost money to cyber fraud, contact your bank promptly and report it through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or helpline 1930.
  • Elsewhere. Use your mobile carrier’s reporting channel and the national telecom or fraud reporting service for your country.

How to reduce exposure of your own number

Review the public contact details in profiles and listings you control. If a people-search site publishes your number, follow its official opt-out procedure. For copies in search results or other listings, follow the personal information removal guide. If someone is posting your details to threaten you, see how to document and report doxxing.
