Synthient aggregated records from multiple sources during 2025. Have I Been Pwned describes a processing step that matters when reading the headline. After the data had been normalized and duplicates removed, approximately 183 million distinct email addresses remained. That number counts addresses across the collection. It gives no device count and no count of successful sign-ins. The date HIBP added the collection, 21 October 2025, is a publication milestone. It cannot date an individual exposure. HIBP catalogue entry for Synthient Stealer Log Threat Data.

Suppose you own a mailbox and use it at three online shops. If that address appears against all three shops, it can contribute one to the distinct-address total while the website associations still identify three accounts worth checking. If two addresses forward to your inbox, both could contribute to the total even though you control them. These examples explain how the count works. The published summary gives no breakdown of how frequently either situation occurred.

The website associated with a record can help you decide what to check first. From a trusted device, go directly to a service you recognize and inspect its recent sessions, recovery details and sign-in notifications. Replace an exposed or reused password, revoke unfamiliar sessions and enable stronger sign-in protection where available. For a work account, the account owner and the device administrator may have different pieces of evidence. Share the collection name and the time you received the alert without including any password.

Readers comparing this entry with the ALIEN TXTBASE collection should keep the counts separate. Each publisher reports distinct addresses within its own dataset. Calculating how many distinct addresses appeared across both would require knowing their overlap. Neither catalogue summary gives that figure. Adding the two headline counts would count any shared address twice.
