One headline number, three entirely different populations. A few hundred writers and editors had IP addresses and physical addresses exposed. Tens of thousands of subscribers had names, phone numbers and passwords in plain text. The remainder is a far larger list of email addresses. Averaging those into a single figure hides the fact that the worst outcome here belongs to the smallest group.

The staff exposure is not a fraud problem. Home addresses of named writers, published alongside a defacement of the site itself, is targeting, and that is true of any newsroom's staff whatever the outlet publishes and whoever objects to it. Financial advice does not help with it; the risk is physical, and the remedies are the ones that apply to doxxing rather than to identity theft.

For subscribers, passwords stored in plain text in 2024 means exactly what it says: no hashing failed, because none was attempted. Change that password anywhere it was reused, immediately. And there is a quieter exposure with no remedy at all, appearing on the subscriber list of a political publication discloses something about you that cannot be reset, and that many readers would not have chosen to publish.
