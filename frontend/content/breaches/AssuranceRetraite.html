The people in this set were assistance beneficiaries of France's national pension fund, and the record makes their circumstances plain: income levels and marital statuses sit alongside names, addresses, phone numbers and French social security numbers (NIR). It is hard to design a better list for fraud aimed at vulnerable people.

The field that stands out is the contact details of relatives or other nominated contacts. That turns one person's record into a small family tree, and it enables the scam where a caller claims to be from the pension fund, already knows the beneficiary's details, and asks a son or daughter to help "update the file" or pay a fee to keep the benefit.

The cause, a compromised partner account, is also typical. Large public bodies increasingly share data with service partners, and the partner is often the weaker link. If you or a relative receive support from l'Assurance retraite, treat any call asking for bank details or a fee as fraud. Hang up and call back on the number from an official letter you already have.
