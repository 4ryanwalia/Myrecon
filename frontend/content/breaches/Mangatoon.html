Two things in this record matter more than the 23 million total. The first is salted MD5 password hashing, which is a 1990s answer to a 2022 problem: the salt prevents one cracking run solving every shared password, but MD5 is fast enough that ordinary hardware grinds through common passwords regardless. Anything that appears in a wordlist should be considered recovered.

The second is the auth tokens from social logins. Those are not passwords, they are the credentials a site holds so it can act against the account you signed in with. Their value to an attacker depends entirely on what scope was granted and whether the tokens are still valid, which is why "revoke third-party app access" belongs on the response list alongside "change your password". Most people have never opened that settings page and would be surprised by what is on it.

The note that the company did not respond to repeated contact attempts is worth registering too. A breach where the operator is unreachable means no notification, no forced password reset, and no invalidation of those tokens, so the only person who can act is the user. If you had an account here, go into the social account you used to sign in and revoke the connection yourself.
