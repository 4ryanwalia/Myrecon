Nobody in this file signed up for anything. DemandScience is a business-to-business marketing data company: the records were aggregated from public and commercial sources into contact profiles, and the people described by them were never customers. The exposure is a by-product of an industry that treats professional identity as inventory.

The record also notes the source — a decommissioned legacy system. That is the most preventable failure mode in this entire archive. A system that has been switched off in the organisational sense but not the technical one has no owner, no patching schedule and no monitoring, while still holding a complete copy of the data it was retired with. Every large company has one.

What makes this data valuable is not its sensitivity but its precision. A name, a corporate email address, a job title and an employer is the exact recipe for business email compromise: it identifies who approves payments, who reports to whom, and who would plausibly receive an instruction from a senior colleague. If your details are here, the risk lands at work rather than at home — verify payment and account-change requests through a channel you initiated, every time, regardless of how convincing the sender looks.
