Two data classes, but one of them is "email messages", and that is the part that matters. An email address list tells you who was on a mailing list. Hundreds of gigabytes of messages and attachments tell you what people wrote to a political party, which under European data protection law counts as special category data because it can reveal political opinions. Have I Been Pwned flags this entry as sensitive.

The publication format is new and worth watching. The data was released with searchable, AI-generated summaries alongside the original files. That lowers the effort needed to find a named person in a leak from days of reading to a single search, which makes the exposure for anyone who merely wrote in, including people who are not members, far greater than the headline count of addresses suggests.

The party attributed the breach to an attack on its hosting provider. Whatever one thinks of the organisation, the lesson is general: correspondence sent to any campaign, charity or advocacy group sits on someone else's servers indefinitely. If you are in this set, assume messages you sent could be quoted back to you, and treat any contact that references them with caution.

HIBP marks this record as sensitive, which means it is not publicly searchable in the usual way: an address can only be checked by its verified owner. That design decision exists for exactly this kind of data, where simply being in the file says something about a person's political contact. It is also why this archive page avoids any detail about individual correspondents and limits itself to what the record says about the incident.

The timeline in the record, detection on 9 September and a second intrusion five days later, describes a problem that recurs across this archive: an organisation that discovers an intrusion does not always succeed in removing the intruder before they return. For anyone who handles constituent or member correspondence, the lesson is to keep less of it for less time. Mail that has been deleted under a retention policy cannot be published, summarised or searched by anyone.
