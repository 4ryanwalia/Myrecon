Eighteen data classes is a lot, and the list reads like the fields of a fundraising database, because that is what it came from: alumni engagement and donor records. Charitable donations, family members' names, ethnicities, places of birth, citizenship statuses and marital statuses are not things a university needs to teach anyone. They are things a development office collects to decide whom to ask for money, and how much.

That is the uncomfortable point about this breach. The most detailed profiles belong to the people with the closest relationship to the institution: donors, alumni and parents. Donation history in particular is a ready-made target list, because it says who gives, how much and to what, which is exactly what a fake charity appeal or a fake "alumni office" call needs.

Harvard's notice describes the data as relating to fundraising and alumni engagement. If you are a Harvard alum or donor, expect approaches that cite real gifts, real classmates or real family members. A genuine development office will never object to you hanging up and calling the number on the university's own website.

The field list also includes citizenship statuses and places of birth, which are unusual in any breach and sensitive in a particular way for international students and alumni. Fraud aimed at people with visa or immigration concerns often impersonates officials and uses fear rather than greed, and a caller who knows where someone was born, what they studied and where they now work can sound very official indeed. Anyone in that position should know that immigration authorities do not demand payment over the phone or by gift card.

The year between the November 2025 breach and the November 2026 HIBP date also matters: donation records, event attendance and family details may have been used for targeting before most alumni could check whether they were included. Universities send many genuine fundraising messages, so a useful habit is to make any donation through the giving page you reach yourself, not through a link or phone number supplied by a caller or an email, however much it seems to know.
