One phrase in the field list deserves a stop: finance pre-qualification application data. Those are people who asked a car marketplace what they could borrow, a question nobody asks idly, and the answer is sitting beside their name, phone number and home address.

Intent is the part of a breach that rarely makes the summary and reliably makes the fraud. Knowing that someone is shopping for a car, and roughly what they can finance, tells a caller which pretext will land: a rate they have been approved for, a deposit to hold a vehicle, a dealer following up on the application they really did fill in last week. Generic scams get hung up on. This one continues a conversation the victim started.

Dealer account and subscription records were in the same release, which hands the same caller both ends of that conversation, the buyer's details and a real dealership to impersonate. The defence is procedural rather than technical: anything about a vehicle purchase, a finance offer or a deposit gets confirmed by ringing the dealer on a number you looked up yourself. Never the number in the message, however well the message knows you.
