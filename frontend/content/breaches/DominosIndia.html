An order history attached to a delivery address and a phone number is not retail data, it is a behavioural record. Where you live, roughly when you are home, how often you order, how much you spend and how many people you are ordering for. Twenty-two and a half million of those, from a company most customers thought of as a way to get dinner.

What made this breach notorious in India was what happened after it: a public search interface was built on top of the data, so that anyone could type a phone number and read back the orders and addresses attached to it. That step is the one worth remembering. A dump on a forum has a small audience with some technical patience; a search box has everybody. The same records become a different kind of danger when the barrier drops from "can navigate a hacking forum" to "can type".

The fraud that follows is the refund call, and it works because the caller opens with a real order. Something went wrong with your last delivery, we are processing a refund, approve the request on your phone. No merchant in India or anywhere else returns money by asking you to approve a UPI collect request or read out a one-time code, a refund travels back along the payment that made it, with nothing required from you.
