Nobody in this file has a relationship with the company named at the top of it. Personal details were collated from public sources into Bureau van Dijk's Orbis product, Orbis was licensed to customers, and the leak came from one of those customers. Three hops between the person and the breach, and the person chose none of them.

That is the structural failure of the data broker economy rather than a lapse by anyone in particular. Aggregation is the entire product: a name in one register, a date of birth in a filing, a job title on a third site are individually public and individually useless, and combining them into a profile is what the customer is paying for. Once assembled, the profile copies perfectly and ends up exactly as secure as the least careful licensee. The vendor can be faultless and the outcome identical.

Which is why "sourced from public information" reassures less than it is meant to. Nothing here can be withdrawn at source, because none of it was private to begin with, and there is no account to close, no consent to revoke, and no notification obligation to anyone whose dossier was compiled without their involvement. The exposure is the collation, and the collation is the business.
