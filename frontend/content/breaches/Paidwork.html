Fourteen categories of data in one record, including bank account numbers, passwords and financial transactions, is close to the worst combination a single breach can produce. It is also an unsurprising one for a gig-economy platform: paying people requires banking details, verifying them requires identity data, and running the marketplace generates a behavioural record on top.

The four-month gap in this record is the part to sit with. Data was claimed and listed for sale in March; it was published in July. For anyone in the file, exposure began at the earlier date — every warning, every reset, every "we are investigating" statement in the interim was issued about data that was already in someone else's hands.

The order of response matters here more than usual. Bank details cannot be changed as easily as a password, so start by watching the account rather than trying to fix it: unexpected small transactions are the standard test before a larger one. Then kill the password everywhere it was reused. People who earn through platforms like this are also disproportionately targeted by "your payout failed" messages, which work because they arrive at a moment the recipient is genuinely expecting money.
