Double Counter's own site describes it as a Discord bot that blocks alternate accounts by comparing device, browser and network data, so the data it holds is, by design, the data that links accounts together. That is what makes a leak of an anti-alt service different from an ordinary one: the pairing of an email address with a Discord username is exactly what someone running an alternate or anonymous account would least want published.

The reported cause is also worth noticing. The breach was attributed to a vulnerability in Metabase, an analytics dashboard. Internal tools like this are often left reachable from the internet with more access to raw data than any public page, and they tend to be patched last because nobody thinks of them as part of the product.

For most people in this set the practical risk is targeted phishing on Discord itself: messages that use your real username and mention the server-verification bot you remember using. Discord staff and verification bots do not ask for your password or a login code. The small number of paying subscribers whose names, countries and postcodes were included carry more risk than the rest, because those details connect a gaming identity to a real person.
