This entry is not a company that lost its customers' data. It is the other side of the transaction: a remote access trojan, taken down by Europol and its partners, whose infrastructure held what it had collected from infected computers. If your address is in here, the likely story is not that a website you trusted was breached. It is that a machine you used, or one an account of yours was signed into, was under someone else's control.

That changes the advice. Changing one password is the response to a site breach. A RAT sees everything typed on the infected machine, so every password used there should be treated as known, including ones for sites that were never breached at all. The 893,000 unique passwords Europol shared are now in Pwned Passwords, which is the quickest way to check whether yours is among them.

The order matters. Clean or replace the infected device first, then change passwords from a different, trusted device, starting with email, because email resets everything else. Changing passwords on a machine that is still compromised just hands over the new ones.
