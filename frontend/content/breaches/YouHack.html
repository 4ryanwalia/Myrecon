A hacking forum getting hacked is a familiar story, but the numbers here are the interesting part. YouHack had about 107,000 members. This entry lists 7.2 million email addresses. The difference is the forum's own content: members posted email and password pairs taken from other victims, so most people in this set were never members. They were the material being traded.

That makes it two breaches in one. For the members, the exposure is their email, username, IP address and an MD5 password hash, mostly unsalted, which modern hardware reverses quickly for anything but a long random password. For everyone else, it means a password of theirs was already circulating among people who collect them by 2013.

Old does not mean harmless. Password reuse is a habit, and a password from 2013 is often a close variant of one still in use today. If your address is here, check whether you still use that password, or a pattern built from it, anywhere. Our password leak check guide explains how to test it without sending the password itself.

The gap between the breach date and the HIBP date is one of the longest in this archive. The forum was breached in May 2013 and the data was added in November 2026, more than thirteen years later, as part of a larger redistributed corpus. That timeline is typical of old forum breaches: the data circulates privately for years, is bundled into compilations, and only becomes searchable when one of those compilations reaches someone who reports it.

For the forum's own members, the IP addresses are the field with the longest tail. A member of a Russian-language hacking forum in 2013 may have very good reasons not to want that association surfacing now, and an IP address from that period can still be tied to an ISP and a region. Anyone who used the forum under a username they still use elsewhere should treat that handle as linked to this record.
