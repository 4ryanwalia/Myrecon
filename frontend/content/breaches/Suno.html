Eight months passed between the breach date on this record and the point at which it came to light. That gap is the most useful thing on the page. Everyone in the file spent most of a year with no reason to change anything, while the data was already out, which is why "there is no evidence of misuse" is a statement about what the company can see, not about what has happened.

The second detail worth noticing is the shape of the exposure. Fifty-five million email addresses with names and phone numbers is a contact list. The tens of thousands of payment records inside it are a different category entirely: a much smaller group, exposed far more seriously, buried inside a number so large that the distinction disappears from most coverage.

If you only ever generated a few tracks and never paid, your realistic risk is targeted phishing that references a service you actually used. If you bought anything, you are in the smaller set, check the card statement for that period specifically, and treat any message about a subscription or refund as unverified until you have opened the account yourself.
