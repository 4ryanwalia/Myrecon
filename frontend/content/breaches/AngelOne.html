Angel One's statement that the breach "has no impact on client securities, funds, or credentials" is probably accurate as far as it goes, and it misses the point. Nobody needs your trading password to misuse this data. The set pairs names, phone numbers and addresses with bank account numbers, PANs and portfolio holdings, which is a complete brief for an investment scam.

India's stock-market fraud of the moment is the fake trading group: a WhatsApp or Telegram contact posing as a broker's research desk, offering tips or an "institutional" app, then taking deposits. A caller who can quote your real holdings and your PAN is far more persuasive than one guessing, and this leak supplies exactly that.

Note the dates too. The breach happened in April 2023 and was confirmed in July 2024, more than a year later. The data had plenty of time to circulate before anyone was told. If you held an Angel One account in that period, treat any unsolicited contact about your portfolio as fraudulent unless you started it, and never move money to an account a caller gives you, however much they know.
