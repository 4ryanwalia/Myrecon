The detail worth understanding here is how the card numbers were stored: as unsalted SHA-1 hashes. That sounds protective, and for passwords it would at least slow an attacker down. For card numbers it does very little. A card number is a short string with a known structure: the first six digits identify the issuer, the last digit is a checksum, and only the digits in between vary. That is a small enough space to hash every possibility and match the results, so a hashed card number should be treated as an exposed one.

The rest of the record is a loyalty program's view of a person: phone number, date of birth, email, membership dates and which partner offers they redeemed bonus points on. Loyalty data looks trivial but it is behavioural, and it is the kind of detail that makes a fake bank call sound real ("we noticed an unusual redemption on your Spasibo account").

The data was published in March 2023 and reached Have I Been Pwned in November 2026, so it has had years to be used. If you are a Sberbank customer in this set, the card you held then should be reissued if it has not been already.
