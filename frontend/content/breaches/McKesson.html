Read the data classes rather than the headline. "Personal health data" sitting next to names, dates of birth, employers and addresses is the combination that makes a breach at a distributor behave like a breach at a hospital, and McKesson is a wholesaler, not a care provider. Most of the people in this file have never knowingly had a relationship with it.

That is the pattern worth internalising about healthcare supply chains. The companies holding your health data are rarely the ones you chose: they are the clearing houses, distributors, benefit administrators and billing intermediaries that sit behind the clinic. You cannot audit them, opt out of them, or in most cases name them.

The practical consequence is narrow and specific. Health-adjacent detail is what makes a fraudulent call convincing, a caller who knows your employer, your date of birth and something about your medication has not proved they are your pharmacy. Treat any unexpected contact referencing your care as unverified, and go back through a number you already had.
