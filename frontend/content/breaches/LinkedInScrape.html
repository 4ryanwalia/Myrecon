Have I Been Pwned describes this collection as scraped public profile data, not a breach of private LinkedIn data. That distinction matters when explaining how the records were obtained, even though their collection and circulation can still create privacy concerns.

The profile you made public was made public one reader at a time. That is a different thing from public data collected at scale and circulated. HIBP's record describes approximately 400 million records and 125 million unique email addresses; the two counts are not counts of individual people. Aggregated contact and employment details can help make phishing messages more targeted.

This is the same argument that runs through the 2021 Facebook scrape and the Clearview AI case file. Public and harmless are not the same word, and scale is what separates them.

The field list explains why this collection keeps reappearing in other compilations. Job titles, education levels and locations are exactly what a recruiter, a salesperson or an attacker wants when deciding who to approach and how. A file that ties those to an email address lets someone sort 125 million addresses by seniority, sector and city, which is the first step in picking a target for a fake job offer, an invoice fraud aimed at a finance team, or a message that pretends to come from a colleague.

That use is the realistic risk for most people in it. The well-worn pattern after professional-network scrapes is a recruiter message for a role that matches your title a little too well, followed by a request to move the conversation to another app, open a document, or complete a "skills test" that installs something. Because the details in the approach are accurate, they read as research rather than as a warning sign.

Unlike most entries in this archive, part of the fix is in your own hands, because the source was profile data you chose to publish. LinkedIn's privacy settings let you limit what is visible to people outside your network and to search engines. Narrowing that does nothing about copies already circulating, but it does reduce what the next scrape collects, and the next one is the one you can still influence.
