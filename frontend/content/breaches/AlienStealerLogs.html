This is not a company's breach and reading it as one gets the response wrong. Stealer logs come from malware running on individual people's own computers, recording credentials as they are typed. If your address appears here, no service failed you. A machine you used was infected, and everything entered on it during that period should be assumed captured.

What makes a stealer log more dangerous than an ordinary credential dump is the third column. A leaked password list tells an attacker a password you once used. A stealer log tells them the password, the website it was entered into, and the email address that goes with it, a working login rather than a guess. Credential stuffing becomes unnecessary when the file already says where the key fits.

So the action here is different from every other entry in this archive. Changing one password is not the fix. Clean the device first, because otherwise the new password is captured too, then rotate credentials starting with email and anything financial.

The source is worth noting too. The record says the logs were obtained from a Telegram channel, which is now a common distribution route for stealer output: operators sell or give away bulk text files to attract buyers, and the files are copied between channels faster than anyone can track. HIBP made this data searchable by the domain of the target website as well as by email domain, which means a company can see which of its own login pages appear in infected users' logs, even when the infected machine belonged to a customer and not to the company.

If you run a service, that search is worth doing. A cluster of your customers' credentials in stealer logs is not a breach of your systems, but it predicts account takeovers that will look like one from the outside. Forcing resets for the affected accounts, and offering stronger sign-in options such as passkeys, closes that gap before it becomes a support queue. If you are an individual, the signs worth acting on are browser extensions or "cracked" software you installed around the time the logs were collected, because that is how most stealer infections begin.
