The important boundary in this record is a marketing platform. Have I Been Pwned attributes the customer-data exposure to an attack on Mindbox and describes records spanning 2018 to August 2024. A person can appear in an old marketing dataset even if they no longer use the restaurant's account or app. The addition date in a breach index is also different from the date the incident occurred.

The source lists names, contact details, birthdays and approximate location, and reports the company's statement that payment and passport details were not included. That statement should not be expanded into either a guarantee that no other risk exists or a claim that cards were stolen. The listed fields can help make a fake loyalty reward or account-support message sound personal. This is a risk interpretation, not a report of an observed follow-on scam.

If you receive an unexpected reward or account message, open the official app or a known website yourself. A correct name or birthday does not authenticate the sender. Do not send a one-time code in reply or enter payment details through the message. Report suspicious communication using the service's official support channel and your normal email or device reporting controls.

For a company reviewing its own exposure, this case illustrates why account deletion and marketing-data retention need separate questions. Ask which provider holds historical contact records, what the retention policy covers and how a deletion request propagates to that provider. These are review questions, not a claim about the current Mindbox configuration. The available description does not give the exact intrusion mechanism, so this article leaves that cause unresolved.
