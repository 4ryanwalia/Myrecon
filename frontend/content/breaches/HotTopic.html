Fifty-seven million email addresses out of a mall clothing retailer. The figure is wildly out of proportion to the business, and that is the first thing to notice: selling t-shirts does not require a permanent record of who bought what, when, and on which birthday. A loyalty programme is a data business wearing a discount, and the data outlives the customer relationship by years.

The combination here is a working phishing kit rather than a fraud kit. Name, salutation, date of birth, purchase history, and the card type, expiry and last four digits. None of it can be spent. All of it can be recited, and reciting your last order and the end of your card number is precisely what most people accept as proof that a caller is genuinely from the shop or the bank.

That test has not worked for some time, and breaches like this one are why. Partial card data has no value except in being convincing. Nobody legitimate establishes their identity by reading your own card back to you and then asking for the rest of the number, the expiry date, or a code that has just arrived by text. The institution that issued the card already knows what is on it.
