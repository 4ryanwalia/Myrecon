A data centre operator's contact list is a map of who runs other companies' infrastructure. The people in this set are not random consumers: they are users of the platform, sales leads and CyrusOne employees, which means facilities managers, network engineers and the people who can authorise physical or remote access to servers.

The field that makes this more than a contact list is support tickets. Tickets at a data centre are where customers describe access requests, hardware changes and outages in their own words, sometimes naming the systems involved. Like any support record, they also capture whatever someone typed while trying to get a problem fixed quickly.

The realistic follow-on attack is social engineering aimed at the help desk, from either direction: someone posing as a customer to request access, or posing as CyrusOne staff to ask a customer to "confirm" credentials. If you manage infrastructure hosted there, treat any unexpected access or credential request as suspect until it is verified through a channel you set up before this leak, and check whether your old tickets mention anything that should be rotated.
