Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 September 2026 · Applies to myrecon.xyz and the MyRecon Android app

The short version

  • Accounts are optional. Guests can see a 100-platform preview from a 561-platform username sweep. If you sign in with Google, we hold your email, name, scan counts and saved username scans, which you can delete. We never see card, UPI or bank details: Razorpay handles payment.
  • Guest scans are not saved to an account. Results can sit in a short-lived server memory cache for up to ten minutes. Completed username scans while signed in are saved to your account history.

  • Passwords never leave your device. The password checker hashes locally and sends five characters of that hash.
  • We never sell data, never profile visitors, and never pass what you type to any advertising system.

3. The password checker

The password tool is the one place where you might type a genuine secret, so it is built so that the secret cannot reach us even if we wanted it to.

  • The password is hashed in your browser with SHA-1, using the browser’s own cryptography.
  • Only the first five characters of that hash are sent, to the Pwned Passwords range API at api.pwnedpasswords.com. That prefix matches hundreds of different passwords, so it identifies nothing.
  • The response is padded, so even its size gives nothing away. The match is worked out on your device.
  • The password reaches neither MyRecon’s servers nor Have I Been Pwned. It never leaves your browser.
  • This tool runs entirely client-side. It is excluded from history, from saved investigations, from shareable links and from exports, so a typed password cannot be written anywhere by accident.

This technique is called k-anonymity. Have I Been Pwned receives your IP address, as it would for any request your browser makes to it.

4. Forms and the email links

The breach-check box

Breach articles carry a box asking for an email address. Submitting it does not send the address to us as a form: the page hands it to the lookup tool on the home page, in your browser, and the tool then runs an ordinary email lookup, processed exactly as section 2 describes and not stored. Because it is passed in the address bar, the address may appear in your browser history. Clear your history if that matters to you, or use the tool on the home page directly.

These boxes ask for an address and nothing else. We recommend you only check addresses that are yours.

The email links

There is no contact form anywhere on this site. Every “contact” control is a plain mailto: link that opens your own mail application with the subject and questions pre-filled. Nothing is transmitted until you press send in your own mail client, and what you send travels through your mail provider to our mailbox. You control exactly what goes in it.

Our interest is in a service that stays up and is not used to attack anyone; the data involved is minimal and is not used to profile you.

Storing your theme, history and saved investigations in your browser is done on the basis that you asked for it: it is a preference you set and a feature you used, and nothing is transmitted to us.

8. How long anything is kept

  • Guest search queries and results: up to 10 minutes in an in-memory cache, then discarded; no saved guest scan history.
  • Signed-in username scans: saved to your account until you delete individual scans, clear your scan history or request account deletion.
  • IP address for rate limiting: 60 seconds.
  • Server logs: the standard short retention of our hosting providers, used for security and diagnostics and not to profile visitors.
  • App invite records: kept for as long as the invite programme runs, because they are what stops the same phone being counted twice, and deleted when it ends.
  • Accounts: until you ask us to delete yours. Deletion removes the account and its usage counts; payment records we are required to keep are retained for the legal period.
  • Payment records: as long as Indian tax and accounting law requires, currently up to 8 years.
  • Guest scan counters: two days.
  • Browser storage: until you clear it. We cannot clear it for you, and we cannot read it.
  • Email correspondence: as long as needed to deal with the matter, and for a reasonable period afterwards as a record.

10. Security

The measures that matter most here are architectural: guest lookups do not create account history, signed-in username scans are scoped to your account, and the password checker never sends us the password you type.

  • The site is served over HTTPS and sends HSTS, including subdomains, with preload. API requests are HTTPS-only.
  • The API is rate-limited; guest scans are not saved as account history.
  • The password checker is built so a password cannot reach our servers at all.
  • Credentials for third-party services are held as environment variables, never in the codebase.

No system is perfectly secure. If you believe you have found a vulnerability, please report it privately to aryan@bugsnaps.in before disclosing it publicly; see our contact page.

16. The Android app

This policy is also the privacy policy for the MyRecon Android app. The app is built on a different principle from the website: the work happens on your handset rather than on our servers, so for most of what it does there is no MyRecon server in the picture at all.

  • Username sweeps run from your device. Your handset contacts the platforms being checked directly, so those platforms see your device’s IP address rather than a server’s. That is deliberate, it is why the results are better, but it is a trade-off you should know about.
  • Breach alerts do not require your address. The app downloads the public breach catalogue, from haveibeenpwned.com and from this site’s own feed, and compares it against your watch list on your device. The address itself is never sent.
  • Checking a specific address does disclose it. That is the trade-off, so it sits behind its own switch with its own warning in the app. The per-address breach check sends the address to leakcheck.io; the in-app email lookup sends it to api.xposedornot.com, as part of the web address, which means it can appear in that provider’s ordinary server logs. Neither runs unless you ask for that address.
  • Password checks work exactly as section 3 describes: hashed on the device, five characters of the hash sent to api.pwnedpasswords.com, the password itself never transmitted.
  • Importing a QR code from a picture happens on your device. The image comes from the system picker, not the camera, and the code is decoded locally. No picture you open in the app is ever uploaded, to us or to anyone else. What the app does next with the link it finds is described in the camera bullet below.
  • Camera permission is for the QR code scanner. The code is read and decoded on your device, and the camera image is never uploaded or saved. Acting on what it finds does use the network: to tell you where a code really leads, the app follows the link’s redirects, so each server in that chain sees a request from your device, and looks the final domain up in the public RDAP registry (rdap.org) to see how recently it was registered.
  • If a source will not answer your phone, that check is retried through us. Some services refuse requests from mobile networks, and rather than show you nothing, the app retries that single lookup through the MyRecon API at myrecon.onrender.com: which means, for that lookup only, the identifier you typed and your device’s IP address reach our server. It applies to the email, domain, DNS and IP tools. We keep no record of it. The username sweep and the on-device tools never take this path.
  • The self-scan reads your own connection. To tell you what your phone reveals, the app asks ipwho.is about the address you are connecting from, which returns your approximate area, provider and whether you are behind a VPN, and resolves names through cloudflare-dns.com. Both see your IP address, as any website you visit does. Nothing about the phone itself is sent to us.
  • Deep Search queries public search engines. Searches run from your handset against lite.duckduckgo.com and www.bing.com, and against the open registries wikidata.org, orcid.org and api.github.com. Each sees the query and your device’s IP address. None of it passes through us.
  • Other lookups reach the services they need. Domain and certificate work uses rdap.org and api.certspotter.com; GitHub lookups use api.github.com. Each receives the identifier you asked about and your device’s IP address.
  • Notification permission is used to tell you when a breach matches something you are watching.
  • Invites are counted with Google Firebase. Deep Search unlocks once three people install the app from your invite link. The link carries an invite code worked out from your phone’s app-specific Android ID; whoever you send it to can use it to see how many people have joined through it, and nothing more. If you install from someone else’s link, the app records in a Firebase Realtime Database, run by Google in Singapore, their invite code, a one-way hash derived from your phone’s app-specific Android ID, so the same phone cannot be counted twice, even after a reinstall, and the time. While the invite card is on screen, the app reads your own count from the same database. No name, email address, account or anything you look up is involved, and if you install without an invite link, nothing about your install is recorded.
  • What the app stores: your watch list, settings and results, is kept on your device in Android’s own app storage, and is removed when you uninstall the app. There is no account and no sync.
