Sherlock Project vs MyRecon for username search

Sherlock Project is the open-source username checker at sherlockproject.xyz. Choose it for local command-line checks and file exports. Use MyRecon for public profile leads in a browser, or Maigret for enriched reports. MyRecon publishes this comparison and ran the linked study.

Choose a browser, terminal or report workflow

MyRecon suits a one-off browser review or a device where you cannot install tools. Sherlock suits local command automation and output files you manage. Maigret documents enriched research reports, a web interface and library use. Each needs source review; a larger advertised catalogue cannot establish who owns a profile.

Reproduce a small browser and local review

Choose a handle you control and a short list of platforms you can check directly. Record the tool version, selected platforms, date, source URL and unresolved responses. Sherlock documents CSV and XLSX output. Its JSON option loads site definitions. A browser preview can limit visible results, so record the scope and access you actually used.

Why Sherlock recorded two fewer supported matches

In our 1 November 2026 study of 35 controls, Sherlock supported four matches. The MyRecon web engine and Maigret each supported those same four plus one on Hacker News and one on Dev.to. Sherlock excluded both platforms in the run, accounting for the full two-match gap in this sample. Its nine excluded controls are counted among its 20 unresolved or unscored results. What to do when tools disagree

Compare the exact profile address, the supported sites and the outcome for each. A policy exclusion means the tool did not check that site. A challenge, login wall or ambiguous response can leave a check unresolved. Deduplicate profile addresses before counting leads and use the provider's account controls to verify ownership.

When MyRecon is useful, and when another workflow fits

Use MyRecon when you remember an old handle and want public leads to compare with your inbox or password manager. The same workflow can support an authorized footprint review, while the separate email tool checks supported breach exposure without spending the username allowance. Keep a local tool when scripting or local file control is the requirement. Neither approach reveals all private profiles, authenticates identity or deletes accounts.

Data handling and limits at the source

Local Sherlock execution still sends requests to third-party sites; MyRecon uses hosted services as described in its privacy policy. Neither is automatically anonymous. Rate limits, login restrictions, changing site rules and provider outages affect what can be observed. Use bounded authorized inputs and keep exports private. A paid scan expands product scope, not your access rights on the source platform.

Frequently asked questions

Is MyRecon a free Sherlock alternative?

It offers free guest Quick previews and a signed-in daily Standard allowance, with optional paid features. Guest results show up to 5 cards in full; see pricing for current limits.

Why did the tools return different match counts?

In this 35-control study, the two additional supported matches came from Hacker News and Dev.to. Sherlock had excluded those sites in the run. The result describes the tested versions and settings.

When is Sherlock a better fit?

Consider Sherlock when you need a local command-line workflow or script integration and can maintain the environment and review the sources.
