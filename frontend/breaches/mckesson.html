MyRecon's take

Read the data classes rather than the headline. "Personal health data" sitting next to names, dates of birth, employers and addresses is the combination that makes a breach at a distributor behave like a breach at a hospital, and McKesson is a wholesaler, not a care provider. Most of the people in this file have never knowingly had a relationship with it.

That is the pattern worth internalising about healthcare supply chains. The companies holding your health data are rarely the ones you chose: they are the clearing houses, distributors, benefit administrators and billing intermediaries that sit behind the clinic. You cannot audit them, opt out of them, or in most cases name them.

The practical consequence is narrow and specific. Health-adjacent detail is what makes a fraudulent call convincing, a caller who knows your employer, your date of birth and something about your medication has not proved they are your pharmacy. Treat any unexpected contact referencing your care as unverified, and go back through a number you already had.

HIBP lists four groups inside the data: marketing campaign recipients, patients, staff and healthcare provider contacts. That mix means two very different fraud risks run in parallel. Patients face health-themed social engineering, such as fake prescription refill calls, fake insurance reviews and fake billing disputes. Provider contacts and staff face business fraud, such as fake purchase orders, fake account changes for supplier payments and requests to "re-verify" access to ordering portals.

The company's notice refers to unauthorised access to certain third-party applications. For a clinic or pharmacy that orders through a distributor, the practical step is to agree a call-back procedure for any change to delivery addresses, bank details or ordering credentials, so that a convincing email carrying real names and account context is not enough on its own to move money or medicine.

The story behind the breach

In August 2026, healthcare and pharmaceutical company McKesson was targeted in a ShinyHunters "pay or leak" extortion campaign . The group subsequently published a substantial corpus of data they alleged was sourced from the company, which included 6.4M unique email addresses among other personal and corporate data attributes. The impacted data related to a range of individuals and roles, including marketing campaign recipients, patients, staff and healthcare provider contacts.

In McKesson's disclosure notice , the company advised it had identified unauthorised access to "certain third-party applications and the exfiltration of certain data was associated with a subset of customers within our Oncology & Multispecialty and Medical-Surgical business units", but had "reasonable assurance of no ongoing unauthorized activity".

What was exposed, and why it matters

  • Dates of birthOne of the three things call centres ask to verify identity, and it never changes.
  • Email addressesAn exposed address may be used in phishing attempts. Treat unexpected messages with caution.
  • EmployersConnects a personal account to a workplace, which is how attackers pivot from a person to a company.
  • GendersSmall on its own; useful to anyone assembling a fuller profile.
  • NamesTurns an anonymous address into an identified person, which is what makes targeted phishing possible.
  • Personal health dataAdds detail to a profile that can be assembled from several breaches at once.
  • Phone numbersEnables SIM-swap attacks against SMS two-factor codes, and puts the number on scam-call lists indefinitely.
  • Physical addressesWhere someone actually lives. Combined with a name this moves the risk off the internet.

What to do if you were in it

  1. Move two-factor authentication off SMS and onto an authenticator app, which a SIM swap cannot intercept.
  2. Be sceptical of post and callers who already know your address, knowing it is no longer evidence of anything.
  3. Be alert to phishing. A message may use details from exposed or collected records to appear familiar; verify requests through the service directly.
