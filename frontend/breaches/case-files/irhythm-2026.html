The confirmed timeline

In its 2 October 2026 update, iRhythm said unauthorized parties accessed and downloaded data from certain third-party-hosted business applications between 3 and 8 June. The company detected the access around 8 June. After investigation and data review, it began notifying affected people with available contact information on 2 October.

The information involved

The company listed patient names and contact information, account and device identifiers, insurance numbers, service dates and birth dates. It said it does not retain individual financial-account or payment-card information and had not identified impact to products, clinical or medical-device systems, or patient safety.

What remains unknown

The reviewed update did not give an affected-person total, name an attacker or explain the initial access method. These fields remain unknown here. A record count of zero in a machine-readable field must not be read as a claim that nobody was affected.

Why the distinction matters

MyRecon analysis: a notification date is a communication milestone. Treating it as the intrusion date creates a misleading picture of recency and can cause readers to count one incident twice. A useful case record preserves the access window, disclosure and notification as separate facts.

Likewise, access to a business application does not by itself establish compromise of every system operated by an organisation. Keep the scope tied to the statement being cited. If a later disclosure expands that scope, update the case with its source and review date rather than quietly rewriting the original account.
