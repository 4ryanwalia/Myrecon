MyRecon's take

The passwords were bcrypt hashes, which is the right answer and means the credential risk here is comparatively low. The exposure that matters is everything else, and it is unusually personal for a breach of this size.

Wattpad is a writing community with a young user base, and the file includes bios, dates of birth, genders, geographic locations and links to users' other social profiles. A bio is freely written text in which people describe themselves, often, at that age, more candidly than they would on any form. Combined with a date of birth and a location, it produces a record that identifies a person who was a minor at the time and says something about who they were.

That kind of data does not age out of being sensitive; it just stops being accurate. Anyone who wrote on the platform as a teenager and has since built a separate adult identity should check whether the linked social profiles in this file still connect the two.

The story behind the breach

In June 2020, the user-generated stories website Wattpad suffered a huge data breach that exposed almost 270 million records . The data was initially sold then published on a public hacking forum where it was broadly shared. The incident exposed extensive personal information including names and usernames, email and IP addresses, genders, birth dates and passwords stored as bcrypt hashes.

What was the outcome?

268,765,495 accounts are listed in this breach. This is a count of accounts, not necessarily individual people.

The reported exposed data includes Bios, Dates of birth, Email addresses, Genders, Geographic locations, IP addresses, Names, Passwords, Social media profiles, User website URLs, Usernames.

What was exposed, and why it matters

  • BiosSelf-written detail that often contains far more identifying information than people remember putting there.
  • Dates of birthOne of the three things call centres ask to verify identity, and it never changes.
  • Email addressesThe address becomes a confirmed, active target, expect more phishing, better aimed.
  • GendersSmall on its own; useful to anyone assembling a fuller profile.
  • Geographic locationsWhere the account was used, which narrows a person down further than most people expect.
  • IP addressesApproximate location at the time of use, and a way to correlate accounts across services.
  • NamesTurns an anonymous address into an identified person, which is what makes targeted phishing possible.
  • PasswordsAnyone reusing this password elsewhere has those accounts exposed too, credential stuffing is automated and tries them all within days.
  • Social media profilesAdds detail to a profile that can be assembled from several breaches at once.
  • User website URLsAdds detail to a profile that can be assembled from several breaches at once.
  • UsernamesA handle links this account to every other place the same handle is used.

What to do if you were in it

  1. Change this password anywhere you reused it, starting with your email account, that is the one that can reset all the others.
  2. Expect better-aimed phishing. A message that already knows your name and where you have an account is the whole point of a breach like this.

When did it happen, and when did it become public?

The breach is dated 29 June 2020. It was published to Have I Been Pwned on 20 July 2020, a gap of 21 days during which the data was already out.

Is the Wattpad breach real?

Yes. Have I Been Pwned lists it as verified, meaning the data was checked against the source rather than taken on trust.

How many people were affected?

268,765,495 accounts. That is roughly everyone in Brazil. That is accounts rather than people, one person often has several.
