On 8 April 2021, LinkedIn Scraped Data (2021) was breached. 125,698,496 accounts were exposed, including education levels, email addresses, genders. MyRecon rates it 64/100, severe. That is roughly everyone in Japan.

MyRecon's take

LinkedIn's position was that this was not a breach, and on the narrow question the company is right: nothing was accessed that was not already public on a profile, and no system failed. The record here says the same thing. It is also, for the person in the file, an entirely academic distinction.

The profile you made public was made public one reader at a time. That is a different thing from 125 million profiles compiled into a single file, sorted by employer and job title, and sold. Aggregation is the step that creates the capability, a recruiter reading your page is the intended use, a targeted phishing campaign against everyone with "finance" in their title at one company is what the compiled version enables.

This is the same argument that runs through the 2021 Facebook scrape and the Clearview AI case file. Public and harmless are not the same word, and scale is what separates them.

The story behind the breach

During the first half of 2021, LinkedIn was targeted by attackers who scraped data from hundreds of millions of public profiles and later sold them online . Whilst the scraping did not constitute a data breach nor did it access any personal data not intended to be publicly accessible, the data was still monetised and later broadly circulated in hacking circles.

The scraped data contains approximately 400M records with 125M unique email addresses, as well as names, geographic locations, genders and job titles. LinkedIn specifically addresses the incident in their post on An update on report of scraped data .

What was the motive?

The published account does not establish the motive. We cannot tell what the attackers intended from the exposed data alone.

Reported demands or attempts to sell data describe what happened, rather than prove every attacker's intention.

What was the outcome?

125,698,496 accounts are listed in this breach. This is a count of accounts, not necessarily individual people.

The reported exposed data includes Education levels, Email addresses, Genders, Geographic locations, Job titles, Names, Social media profiles.

What was exposed, and why it matters

  • Education levelsAdds detail to a profile that can be assembled from several breaches at once.
  • Email addressesThe address becomes a confirmed, active target, expect more phishing, better aimed.
  • GendersSmall on its own; useful to anyone assembling a fuller profile.
  • Geographic locationsWhere the account was used, which narrows a person down further than most people expect.
  • Job titlesIdentifies who has access to what, which is how targets for business email compromise are picked.
  • NamesTurns an anonymous address into an identified person, which is what makes targeted phishing possible.
  • Social media profilesAdds detail to a profile that can be assembled from several breaches at once.
