Steam profile privacy checklist

Your Steam profile can reveal more than a display name: activity, games, inventory, and social context may each have a different audience. A useful self-audit starts with the official Steam privacy settings, then checks the public view of your own profile. This guide explains what to change and what a username search can, and cannot, establish.

Key takeaways

  • Use Steam's own Edit Profile and Privacy Settings page to set the audience for your profile and its individual components.
  • Inspect your own profile while signed out to see what a visitor can view, and record anything a login wall prevents you from checking.
  • Treat a matching handle found through MyRecon as a possible lead; confirm ownership only with independent public evidence.

Open Steam's own privacy controls

In Steam, open your profile, select Edit Profile, and then Privacy Settings. Steam documents Public, Friends Only, and Private as the main profile states, with additional controls for profile components. Decide separately whether game details, friends, and inventory need to be visible for your use of the service. Do not assume changing the headline profile state answers every audience question.

Review the profile as a visitor

Sign out or use a separate browser session to inspect your own profile URL. Note which name, avatar, games, or comments are actually visible without your account. If a page requires login or cannot be reached, record that as unavailable rather than calling it private everywhere. Recheck after changing a setting because cached search snippets and other websites may lag behind Steam.

Check username reuse without conflating accounts

If your Steam handle is also used elsewhere, search that public username with MyRecon and open each original platform page before deciding it belongs to you. Matching text alone does not prove identity. The MyRecon Android app is free and needs no signup, but its Google Play listing is currently in closed testing, so access may be limited until public release.

Keep a short before-and-after record

Record the Steam URL you control, the visibility option you changed, and the date you checked the visitor view. Avoid collecting other players' personal information or using account recovery features to investigate them. If an old copy remains in a search result, address the source page first and then use the search provider's update process if needed.

Check a public username

Use accounts and identifiers you own or are authorized to review. A matching username is a lead, not proof of identity.

Frequently asked questions

Where are Steam profile privacy settings?

Steam's help page directs account owners from their profile to Edit Profile and then Privacy Settings. It describes Public, Friends Only, and Private profile states plus more specific component controls. Check those settings while signed in to your own account.

Does a private Steam profile remove old copies elsewhere?

No setting on Steam can guarantee removal of screenshots, copies, or snippets hosted elsewhere. After changing visibility, inspect your own public profile and treat older third-party results as separate sources that may need their own correction or removal request.

Can MyRecon confirm a Steam user owns another account?

A public username match is a lead, not ownership proof. Open each original result and look for independent, voluntarily published links. If the link is uncertain, leave the accounts unconnected in your notes.
