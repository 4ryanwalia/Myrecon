Reverse Gamertag Lookup: Find the Accounts Behind a Gaming Name

Published 25 November 2026 · 10 min read

A gamertag is a username with extra problems. The same name can belong to different people on Xbox, PlayStation and Steam, most platforms let players rename themselves, and the profile pages that show a gamertag are often run by third-party stat trackers rather than the console maker. This guide explains what a reverse gamertag lookup can honestly find, which sources it relies on for each platform, and how to tell whether two gaming accounts are really the same person.

What a MyRecon gamertag search covers

MyRecon's signed-in Standard scan checks 53 gaming platforms among its 561 sites, and the Extended scan adds 109 more gaming and fan sites from its 3,000+ catalogue. Those include Steam, Xbox (through xboxgamertag.com), PlayStation (through PSNProfiles), Roblox, Minecraft (through NameMC), Fortnite Tracker, Chess.com, Lichess, osu!, Speedrun.com, Faceit and Twitch.

Counts are taken from the platform catalogues the scans run on, read 25 November 2026. A "found" result means a public page answered for that exact name, not that it belongs to the person you have in mind.

What a reverse gamertag lookup can and cannot find

A reverse gamertag lookup takes one name and asks every site it knows whether a public profile exists under it. When it works, it shows where a player uses the same name: a Steam profile, a Twitch channel, a Chess.com account, a speedrun record, a mod site. That is useful for checking whether a teammate is who they say they are, finding your own old gaming accounts, or spotting someone impersonating a streamer.

It cannot see private data. No public lookup returns the email address, real name, phone number or location behind a console account, and a site that promises to is either guessing or selling something else. It also cannot see accounts that have no public page under that name. For several big platforms the only public pages are third-party trackers, such as Fortnite Tracker for Fortnite players, and those only know players that appear in their own data.

Why name changes break reverse lookups

The biggest source of wrong answers in a gamertag search is not a broken checker. It is that the name changed hands.

  • Steam says a SteamID and the account name used to sign in "can not be changed, even by Steam Support staff members", but the player name shown on the profile "can be changed at any time". So the name you saw in a lobby may not be the name in the profile address, and tomorrow it may be someone else's display name too. (Steam Support.)
  • PlayStation lets players change their online ID "as many times as you want", with the first change free, and gives them the option to show the old ID next to the new one for 30 days. After that, the old name can disappear from the profile. (PlayStation Support.)
  • Old names can be claimed again. Where a platform lets a released name be taken by another player, a profile found under that name today may belong to a different person than it did a year ago.

The practical rule: a match on the name proves only that someone uses that name now. To link two accounts to one person, you need something more than the name.

How to run a reverse gamertag lookup

  1. Search the exact name. Enter the gamertag in MyRecon's username search. Spaces and some characters are not allowed in most profile addresses, so also try the name without spaces, with underscores, and in lower case.
  2. Try the variants players actually use. Gamertags collide constantly, so players add numbers, "TTV" for streamers, "x" prefixes or a clan tag. Search the two or three most likely forms.
  3. Open every result. A found result is a public page answering for the name. Read it: the avatar, the bio, the games played, the country if shown, the date joined.
  4. Look for links the player made themselves. Streamers link their Twitch, YouTube and Discord server; Steam profiles often link a channel; speedrun profiles list socials. A link from one account to another, posted by the account owner, is the strongest evidence you will get from public pages.
  5. Compare activity, not just names. The same rare game, the same timezone of activity, the same avatar art or the same writing style across accounts adds up. One matching name does not.

Common reasons people look up a gamertag

Finding your own old gaming accounts

Old gaming accounts are among the most forgotten. Search every tag you have used since you started playing, then close the ones you no longer want and change any reused passwords. The guide to finding and deleting old accounts covers the closing part.

Checking a trade or a teammate

Item trading, account sales and boosting deals attract scams. Before trading, check whether the name has a history: an account created last week with no games and no links is a warning sign. Never move a trade or a payment off the platform's own system because a profile looks convincing.

Spotting a fake streamer or creator

Impersonators copy a streamer's name with one changed letter and message fans with giveaways. Compare the name character by character with the one linked from the creator's verified channel, and see how to report impersonation.

Use it responsibly

Games are where a lot of children spend time online. Do not use a gamertag lookup to find, follow or contact a minor, and do not use it to track down someone who has blocked you. MyRecon only reads public pages, but the results can still be used to harass someone, and that is not what the tool is for. If a player is threatening you, report them through the platform and keep screenshots rather than investigating them yourself.

Common questions

Can I find someone's real name from their gamertag?

Not from the gamertag alone. Console platforms do not publish real names, emails or locations. You can find other public accounts that use the same name, and sometimes a player has linked their own socials, but a public lookup does not reveal private account details.

How do I look up an Xbox gamertag?

MyRecon checks Xbox gamertags through xboxgamertag.com, a third-party site, as part of its scan. These sites only know gamertags they have indexed, so a missing result does not prove the gamertag is unused.

Can I search a PSN ID?

Yes, through third-party trophy sites such as PSNProfiles, which MyRecon checks. Only players who have been synced to that site appear. PlayStation lets players change their online ID, so an old ID may now belong to someone else.

Why does a gamertag show up on sites the person never used?

Two reasons. Common names are used by many different players, and some sites answer with any name you type. MyRecon reports sites known to echo names as unknown rather than found, and checks other uncertain pages against an invented control name, but you should still open each result and check it belongs to the person you mean.
