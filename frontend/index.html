How MyRecon handles data

01

Search Data

MyRecon processes lookups to return the current results. Search inputs and results stay out of account history and server storage.

In-progress lookups use temporary memory. Comparison snapshots you explicitly save and cleanup checklists stay in this browser. IP-based rate limits, daily keyed IP hashes and hosting request logs support abuse prevention.

02

Account Data

Sign-in keeps your account ID, email, display name, creation time, scan allowances, plan/payment references and saved username reports.

We never sell personal data or share it for another party's marketing. Authentication, hosting and payment providers process account data. Google Analytics and advertising may process IP addresses and cookies; our analytics configuration excludes search values.

03

API Lookups

LeakCheck email lookups run over HTTPS, on the fly, without application caching or account database retention.

Other email sources receive the identifiers needed to answer a lookup. Each provider has its own privacy policy. Recent searches and investigations you save can remain in this browser.

How to use it

  1. Enter a handle, full name or exact email you own or have permission to investigate. For names and handles, add context after a comma to improve relevance.
  2. Press Investigate to check public sources. Guest searches can show up to one public finding when a result is available. Sign in with an eligible paid account for full results.
  3. Open each source before making a connection. Check unavailable and partial-source notices; empty results do not establish absence.
What Deep Search can find

Handle searches ask GitHub, Hacker News, Reddit, Bluesky, mastodon.social and DEV for public accounts and recent activity, and Keybase for published account proofs. Name searches ask Wikidata, GitHub's full-name index and ORCID for candidates. Indexed-web queries cover professional pages, social profiles, public discussions, news, research, records and documents.

Instagram and other source limits

Instagram results are indexed profiles, captions, tagged or mentioned pages and external links. Instagram comment bodies, likes and private content are unavailable. Search engines and platforms may block or rate-limit this server; unavailable sources never count as absent.

Google contributor profiles and reviews

Email searches use the exact email to ask the configured public Google source for a contributor profile, then collect available public Maps reviews for that contributor. Review cards can include the place, address, rating, date, text and an owner reply when returned. Coverage counts describe what the source returned and may be partial or capped. Profile edits do not establish online activity, and no returned profile or reviews does not prove that a Google account or activity does not exist.

MyRecon vs Sherlock, Maigret and WhatsMyName

All four look for one username across many websites. They differ in where they run and in what they tell you when a site will not answer.

  • Sherlock is a local command-line tool; its README lists 400+ social networks. Choose it for scripted runs and output files. MyRecon runs the same kind of search in a browser with nothing to install. Sherlock vs MyRecon
  • Maigret checks far more sites (its README lists 5,900, with a default run of the top 500) and builds reports, but also needs Python. Sherlock vs Maigret vs MyRecon
  • WhatsMyName was archived on 9 November 2026, so its 691 site checks are frozen and will drift as websites change. What to use instead of WhatsMyName
  • Email lookups run in the Email tab: reported breaches plus optional checks for services linked to the address. Reverse email lookup

MyRecon reports every check as found, not found or unknown, and an unknown is never counted as "no account". Whichever tool you use, open each match at its source before relying on it.

Common questions

What is a reverse username lookup and how does it work?

A reverse username lookup checks supported public profile pages for a handle. Results are leads, not proof of registration or ownership; review each match at its source. Read our reverse username lookup guide.

What is a digital footprint check?

A digital footprint check audits your public online exposure by scanning registered usernames, checking whether your email was involved in data breaches, and identifying leaked credentials. See our digital footprint audit guide.

More common questions (5)
Does a username lookup confirm availability?

No. MyRecon checks public profile presence. A missing profile does not guarantee that a handle can be registered. Confirm username availability through the official platform signup or account settings flow.

What is a social media username checker?

A social media username checker looks for public profiles using a handle across supported platforms. MyRecon helps you review those profile leads for your own digital footprint or authorized research. Verify important matches at the source.

What can I check with MyRecon?

MyRecon includes public username, email and breach, password exposure, domain registration, DNS, and IP checks. Coverage and access can vary by tool, so use the live interface and current plan page to confirm what is available.

Does a missing username result prove no account exists?

No. A site may block, rate-limit, rename, or restrict a profile, and some services may not be supported. Treat an unavailable or missing result as inconclusive unless the source provides a verifiable reason.

Can MyRecon confirm that a profile belongs to a person?

No. A matching username is only a lead. Confirm important conclusions with independent public evidence and keep uncertain links unconfirmed. MyRecon does not access private accounts or restricted content.
